The meditative kitchen

Curious? Come cook anytime.

Welcome to my kitchen. Cooking has always been my meditative space — a grounding rhythm of aromatic spices, fresh herbs, and thoughtful meal preparation. Here, authentic Indian flavors meet creative multi-cuisine fusion.

Because traditional Indian cooking relies so much on lentils, basmati rice, chickpea flour, and vegetables, preparing rich, comforting meals without allergen worries is more intuitive than you'd think. My goal is to make flavorful home cooking accessible for every family — whether you're avoiding gluten, tree nuts, peanuts, or sesame, or cooking with no restrictions at all.

The meditative cooking journey

Long before it became a website, cooking was the place I went to slow down. Prepping vegetables, tempering spices in hot oil, watching a gravy come together — each step is a small ritual, not a task to rush through.

That's the spirit behind every recipe here: intentional, unhurried, and built to be enjoyed as much in the making as in the eating.

Structured multi-cuisine logic

Over years of cooking, I built a structured framework for applying authentic Indian spice profiles and tempering techniques — tadka, layered aromatics, slow-built masalas — across global formats: tacos, burgers, quesadillas, rice bowls, and more.

The fusion on this site isn't a shortcut. It's Indian technique, applied deliberately, to dishes you already know and love.

Family safety pledge

Every dish on this site balances flavor with real dietary safety. Recipes in the allergy-safe track are built from the ground up to be gluten-free, nut-free, and sesame-free — and they're always labeled clearly as such.

I never state or imply a recipe is allergy-safe unless it carries that explicit label. Whether you need strict allergen-free adaptations or love the classics exactly as they are, everyone is welcome at our table.

Kitchen notes

Five things I've learned along the way

  • Dals are naturally gluten-free

    Lentil dals, chickpea curries, and basmati rice dishes are built around legumes, rice, and vegetables — no gluten to begin with. Just check the spices you temper them with (see the next note).

  • Watch for hidden wheat in hing

    Pure asafoetida resin is gluten-free, but the powdered hing sold in most shops is compounded with wheat flour. Look for a certified gluten-free version.

    Source: Legal Nomads, celiac guide to North India

  • Sunflower seeds replace cashews

    Soaked raw sunflower or pumpkin seeds blend into a velvety, nut-free base for makhani and korma-style gravies.

  • Easy flatbread swaps

    Cornmeal (makki), finger millet (ragi), sorghum, or chickpea flour (besan) all make naturally gluten-free flatbreads. Buy flours labeled gluten-free, since they're often milled alongside wheat.

  • A mild four-spice base

    Equal parts cumin, coriander, fennel, and mustard seed make a gentle blend with no gluten, nuts, or sesame. Mustard is a priority allergen in Canada, so leave it out if anyone at your table reacts to it.

    Source: Health Canada, mustard as a priority allergen

Mastering the swap

  • Soaked raw sunflower or pumpkin seeds replace cashew and poppy seed pastes for creamy gravies.
  • Cornmeal (makki), finger millet (ragi), sorghum, or chickpea flour (besan) replace wheat flatbreads.
  • Store-bought hing (asafoetida) often hides wheat — look for certified gluten-free versions.
